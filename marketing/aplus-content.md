## Shared modules (same on both books — this is the series identity)

### Module 1 — Standard Image Header with Text
**Image slot A:** 970×600. Mango on the riverbank, from either book's opening
spread. No text baked into the image.

> **Feelings are easier to name when you can see them.**
>
> Mango is a little crocodile with bumps along his back that change colour with
> what he feels. Green when he's calm. Yellow when he's worried. Red-hot when
> he's angry. Children who don't yet have words for a feeling can point at the
> colour — and that's where every conversation starts.

### Module 2 — Standard Three Images & Text
**Image slots B, C, D:** 300×300 each. Crops from three interior spreads showing
three different ridge colours (calm green / worried yellow / angry red-orange).

> **A story, not a lecture**
> Mango gets it wrong before he gets it right. The first thing he tries makes it
> worse — because that's what actually happens to children.

> **One technique per book**
> Each story ends with something a child can do: aim the attention, or breathe
> out like blowing candles. Small enough to remember at school.

> **Two pages for the grown-ups**
> A clinical note at the back: what the feeling is, why the instinctive parental
> response backfires, four things that help at home, and when to seek support.

### Module 3 — Standard Single Image & Sidebar
**Image slot E:** 300×400. The feelings colour chart page from the interior.

> **The colour chart**
>
> Green — calm · Yellow — worried · Grey — sad or embarrassed · Red-orange —
> angry · Bright green — proud
>
> Families use it as a shortcut. "What colour are you right now?" gets an answer
> when "what's wrong?" doesn't.

### Module 4 — Standard Text
> **Written by a child & adolescent psychiatrist**
>
> Dr. Zaid Alzureiqat wrote these books after hearing the same question in
> clinic: the diagnosis is explained, the parents nod, and then — "what do we
> actually do at home?" Six-year-olds don't read parenting guides. So the
> technique went into a picture book instead.
>
> For ages 4–8. Read together, or read alone.

## Book-specific module (insert as Module 2, pushing the rest down)

### Book 1 — Mango and the Busy Brain
**Image slot F:** 970×300. The classroom/distraction spread.

> **For the child whose brain runs ahead of the room**
>
> Mango's thoughts move faster than the lesson. He isn't being difficult and he
> isn't not trying — he's aiming at everything at once. Gogo the turtle gives
> him the sentence the book is built on: **don't trap it — aim it.** Attention
> isn't a cage you sit still inside. It's something you point, lose, and point
> again. The pointing-again *is* the skill.

### Book 2 — Mango and the Red-Hot Back
**Image slot G:** 970×300. The peak-anger spread, ridge fully red-orange.

> **For the child who goes from calm to volcano in three seconds**
>
> When Mango is angry, the harder he fights it, the bigger it gets. Gogo tells
> him the thing that actually works: **anger is a wave — you don't fight a wave,
> you let it pass.** Pause. Breathe out like you're blowing out candles. Then
> speak. The book shows him failing at it once before it works.
